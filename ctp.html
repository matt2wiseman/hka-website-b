Car accident injuries (CTP)

If you were injured in a motor vehicle accident in Queensland caused fully or partly by another driver, the at-fault vehicle's CTP insurer may pay for reasonable and appropriate treatment.

Before your first visit

  1. Step 1

    See a doctor

    Get your injuries checked and ask for a medical certificate. You'll need it for your claim form.

  2. Step 2

    Lodge your claim

    Submit a Notice of Accident Claim Form to the CTP insurer of the at-fault vehicle, usually online through the Queensland CTP portal. The sooner you lodge, the sooner treatment can be approved.

  3. Step 3

    Ask for treatment approval

    Request physiotherapy through the CTP portal or by contacting the insurer, or let us request it for you after your first visit.

  4. Step 4

    Book a CTP appointment

    Bring your claim number and the insurer's contact details. Once treatment is approved, we bill the insurer directly.

Claim deadlines

  • Generally within nine months of the accident, or of symptoms first appearing if your injuries weren't obvious straight away
  • Within one month of first meeting a lawyer, if you engage one and that date is earlier
  • Within three months if the at-fault vehicle can't be identified

Different rules apply to children under 18.

How your sessions work

Insurers usually approve treatment in blocks, based on a treatment plan we send them after your assessment. We report progress to the insurer and your GP and request more sessions when they're needed.

Car accidents often affect more than one area, such as the neck, back and a knee. We treat all of it, not only the lower limb, and focus on getting you back to work and everyday life.

Common questions

Do I need a lawyer?
No. You can deal directly with the insurer. Some people choose legal advice if their claim is complex or disputed.
What if the accident was partly my fault?
You may still be able to claim, though any compensation may be reduced. If you were entirely at fault, or no one was at fault (for example, a single-vehicle accident), CTP generally won't cover treatment.
What if I can't identify the other vehicle?
The claim goes to the Nominal Defendant, which acts as the insurer for uninsured and unidentified vehicles. The claim form must be lodged within three months of the accident.
Will I have to pay upfront?
Not for treatment the insurer has approved. Before approval, [CONFIRM your policy].
I have neck pain or whiplash. Can you help?
Yes. Neck and back injuries are common after car accidents, and our physios treat them.
What about serious injuries?
People with eligible serious injuries from Queensland road accidents may be supported by the National Injury Insurance Scheme Queensland (NIISQ), regardless of fault. [CONFIRM whether you'll register as a NIISQ provider.]
